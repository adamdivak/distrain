# Session log — hunting an 8×A100 PCIe box (2026-08-22)

Goal: buy the write-up's largest missing measurement, a **topology-verified A100
PCIe** point on the transport axis. The 8-GPU shape was not available anywhere,
but a **2-GPU secure box appeared late in the session and was measured** — the
first genuine PCIe fabric in this project. An unattended hunter now holds the
line for the 8-GPU version.

**Spend: $1.97** — $1.38 wasted on community pods that never started a container
(three of them our own bug), $0.59 on the box that worked. Nothing is billing
(`verify` clean at the end).

| what | result |
|---|---|
| Prime Intellect 8×A100 PCIe | **does not exist**; every "PCIe" offer is an SXM4 box |
| RunPod 8×A100 80GB PCIe (secure + community) | genuinely PCIe hardware, **no capacity** |
| RunPod 2×A100 80GB PCIe (community) | 4 dead pods, 3 of them our own registry-auth bug |
| RunPod 2×A100 80GB PCIe (**secure**, appeared late) | **measured** — see §6 |

## 1. Prime Intellect cannot supply PCIe, and that is now provable before renting

§22 ended with "do not plan another PCIe session on this venue without a way to
verify the fabric *before* renting", after a `--socket PCIe` offer delivered an
SXM4 box on a full NV12 mesh. That way exists: the offer's **`cloudId`**.

```
A100_40GB | lambdalabs | gpu_8x_a100          | PCIe | us-east-1        | Available | 15.92
A100_40GB | lambdalabs | gpu_8x_a100          | PCIe | me-west-1        | Available | 15.92
A100_40GB | lambdalabs | gpu_8x_a100          | PCIe | europe-central-1 | Available | 15.92
A100_40GB | lambdalabs | gpu_8x_a100          | PCIe | asia-northeast-2 | Available | 15.92
A100_80GB | lambdalabs | gpu_8x_a100_80gb_sxm4| SXM4 | us-east-1        | Available | 22.32
A100_80GB | vultr      | vbm-112c-2048gb-8-a100-gpu | SXM4 | fra        | Available | 22.40
```

`gpu_8x_a100` is lambdalabs' **8×A100-SXM4-40GB** instance type. All four
"PCIe" offers are the same machine in four regions — including the exact box
that failed the topology gate on 2026-08-21. Lambda has no 8-way A100 PCIe
instance at all, so this is a catalogue-level mislabel, not regional bad luck.

`prime_session.py` now carries a `MISLABELLED_FABRIC` map keyed on `cloudId`:
pinning `--socket PCIe` matches **nothing** rather than renting an NVLink mesh,
and `avail` says why. `--socket any` is unaffected — pinning a socket *is* the
fabric claim. The false HIT this used to produce in `watch_capacity.sh` is now
correctly a miss.

## 2. RunPod has the right hardware and no capacity

RunPod sells `NVIDIA A100 80GB PCIe` as its own GPU type — a different SKU from
`NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB`, so the card itself is the PCIe variant and no topology
lie is possible at the catalogue level. Secure $11.12/h, community $9.52/h; both
cheaper than the $22.32/h SXM4 anchor.

Capacity at 8 GPUs was confirmed absent **at the deploy call**, not merely by the
advisory precheck (which has known false negatives, §20):

```
runpod.error.QueryError: There are no longer any instances available with the
requested specifications. Please refresh and try again.
```

Both tiers, and again at 4 GPUs. Only **2 GPUs on community** had stock. A
rejected deploy creates nothing and costs nothing, so this probe is free and is
what the hunter below uses.

## 3. Four dead community pods, $1.38 — and three were our own bug

The 2-GPU point was worth buying on its own: it measures true **GPU-to-GPU PCIe
P2P**, which the write-up explicitly says the forced-TCP/loopback control is *not*
a substitute for. Four attempts on community capacity produced nothing.

| pod | image | start command | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nedtxjwu8kp634` | `distrain:c8c72e1` | `pod-entry.sh` | container created, never ran — abandoned at 23 min, uptime 0 |
| `5x2eyfwlev03sy` | `runpod/pytorch:1.1.0` | *(image CMD)* | EXITED immediately |
| `gxllz4grtoegyv` | `runpod/pytorch:1.1.0` | `/start.sh` | EXITED immediately |
| `57f5o80kilbvjb` | `runpod/pytorch:1.1.0` | `sleep infinity` | **IMAGE_AUTH_ERROR**, pod stopped |

A provider e-mail named the cause for the stock-image pods, and it was ours:

```
IMAGE_AUTH_ERROR: Head "https://registry-1.docker.io/v2/runpod/pytorch/manifests/..."
unauthorized: incorrect username or password
```

`_ensure_template` attached the account's **GHCR** credential to a **Docker Hub**
image. RunPod hands the named credential to whichever registry the image names,
so the wrong one *fails the pull* rather than being ignored. All three
`runpod/pytorch` pods died of this, not of anything about the host. Fixed:
`--registry-auth-name ''` builds a template with no credential, and the template
name gains a `-public` suffix so it cannot be satisfied by reusing the
authenticated one of the same tag.

Pod 1 is unexplained but not evidence of much: a 22 GB private image on an
unknown community host, abandoned at 23 minutes while `uptimeInSeconds` was
still 0. That is consistent with a slow pull. **No conclusion is drawn here
about whether community capacity works** — the earlier draft of this log claimed
one, and the e-mail withdrew it.

Two things were learned the expensive way and are now enforced in code:

- **`desiredStatus: RUNNING` is the pod, not the container.** A pod bills from
  the moment it is rented while its container may be pulling, stuck, or already
  exited; `runtime.uptimeInSeconds` is what separates the three. Watching the
  wrong field is what cost 23 minutes on pod 1.
- **RunPod's SSH proxy is the only way onto a community host.** They expose no
  public port, so `_wait_for_ssh`'s public-port wait can never succeed there.
  `up --ssh-proxy` waits for the container instead and prints
  `ssh -tt <podHostId>@ssh.runpod.io` (the `-tt` is not optional — the proxy
  rejects a session without a PTY, and the error reads like an auth failure).

## 4. What was built, so the next opening is not missed

- **`scripts/pcie_hunt.sh`** — polls RunPod secure by *attempting the deploy*,
  and on success starts `guard`, runs the measurement, pulls the artefacts,
  terminates and verifies. Unattended and cost-capped (1 h / $11.12 ceiling, an
  EXIT trap, `down --yes`, `verify` as the last word). A deploy that succeeds and
  then fails to boot backs off 30 min rather than retrying a broken host in a
  loop.
- **`scripts/pcie_measure.sh`** — the on-pod measurement: topology gate first
  (any `NV#` between GPUs aborts before a number is produced), then roofline,
  `nccl-tests` bandwidth, and batch-480 benches at micro-batch 30 (matching
  `transport.csv`'s existing rows) and 60 (matching the converged run).
- **`scripts/allreduce_bw.py`** — effective bus bandwidth on `nccl-tests`'
  convention in ~90 lines of torch, for a box that cannot boot the image that
  carries `nccl-tests`. Bus bandwidth is rank-normalized, which is why a 2-GPU
  box can speak to an 8-GPU fabric at all; its arithmetic is pinned to the
  anchor's own printed 512 MiB row in `tests/test_allreduce_bw.py`.
- `watch_capacity.sh` gained `WATCH_PRIME_GPUS`, so a PCIe hunt polls both
  memory sizes — the socket is what is scarce, not the capacity of the card.

## 5. The measurement — 2×A100 80GB PCIe, secure, $0.59

Secure 2-GPU capacity appeared in CA-MTL-3 after the 8-GPU probes had come up
empty. Pod `yomp8hs4bn1ovz`, $2.78/h, 19:33–19:47 UTC, image `c8c72e1`.

**The topology gate passed** — and this is the first time it has:

```
        GPU0    GPU1    CPU Affinity    NUMA Affinity
GPU0     X      PHB     0-251           0
GPU1    PHB      X      0-251           0
```

`PHB` is a connection traversing PCIe and the host bridge. No `NV#` anywhere.

**P2P is not available on this box, and that is the headline.**
`nvidia-smi topo -p2p r` reports `CNS` (chipset not supported) for both
directions, and NCCL routes every channel `via SHM/direct/direct`:

```
NCCL INFO P2P is disabled between connected GPUs 0 and 1.
NCCL INFO Channel 00 : 0[0] -> 1[1] via SHM/direct/direct
```

So the traffic is staged through **host memory**, not sent GPU-to-GPU across
PCIe. This is not a fallback we chose — it is what the rentable node offers. Any
"PCIe P2P" reading of these numbers would be wrong; what they measure is *what
renting an A100 PCIe node actually delivers*.

| measurement | value |
|---|---|
| Effective all-reduce bus bandwidth | **2.29 GB/s** (8 MiB–512 MiB, `all_reduce_perf`) |
| Measured bf16 peak | **256.5 TFLOP/s** at n=4096; **223.9** at n=16384 |
| 2 GPU, compiled `ddp_torch` | **1745.4 ms** ± 19.6 |
| 2 GPU, compiled `ddp_interleaved` | 1869.3 ms ± 13.3 |
| 2 GPU, uncompiled `ddp_interleaved` | 2865.4 ms ± 17.3 |
| 2 GPU, uncompiled `ddp_torch` | 2860.6 ms ± 21.6 |
| 2 GPU, compiled `ddp_torch`, micro-batch 60 | 1700.5 ms ± 18.2 |
| 1 GPU, same box | **2786.8 ms** ± 10.7 |

All arms hold global batch 480 at micro-batch 30 — `transport.csv`'s existing
rows' chunking — so only the rank count and the fabric differ from them.

**Three results fall out.**

1. **Scaling at 2 ranks over this fabric is 79.8%** (1.597× on 2 GPUs), against
   95.8% for 8 ranks over NVLink. The comparison is not like-for-like in rank
   count, but it is the same batch and chunking on one box.
2. **Compilation is worth 1.64×; overlap is worth nothing.** §21 predicted the
   compile-vs-overlap lead would *dissolve* as bandwidth fell. On real hardware
   it is the **overlap** difference that dissolves — the two uncompiled modes
   land within 0.2% of each other — while compilation stays decisive, and among
   compiled modes `ddp_torch` still leads interleaved by 7.1%. Rendered as
   [`pcie_modes.svg`](../plots/pcie_modes.svg).
3. **The PCIe card throttles where the SXM4 cards do not**: 256.5 TFLOP/s at
   n=4096 falls to 223.9 at n=16384, while both SXM4 cards peak *at* n=16384.
   The datasheet 312.0 that `mfu.py` had been carrying as `UNVERIFIED` is 22%
   above the best sustained figure. `measure_roofline.py` had also been printing
   `16384^3` into its provenance line unconditionally; it now names the size the
   peak actually came from, because on this card that is n=4096.

**Projected to 8 ranks** by the standard reconstruction (`anchor_compute +
comm`, comm from the measured 2.29 GB/s): **826 ms/step → 2.29 h to 3.28**, or
**2.44× slower than NVLink** and **1.54× faster than the forced-TCP control**,
against 7.19 h for a single A100. An 8-GPU PCIe server's ring crosses more host
bridges than this 2-GPU box's does, so treat it as an optimistic bound.

## 6. Where this leaves the write-up

The transport axis now has a point measured on **hardware people can actually
rent** rather than on netem, sitting between NVLink and the TCP/loopback
control. The draft's own caveat — that the forced-socket control "is not a
substitute for direct GPU-to-GPU PCIe P2P" — turns out to understate the
situation: on the rentable PCIe node, direct P2P *is not on offer at all*.

The **8-GPU** bar is still missing, and its `data_gaps.csv` entry is now sharper
than "the provider mislabelled it": **the hardware exists at one venue, at half
the SXM4 anchor's price, and had no 8-GPU capacity on 2026-08-22.** That is a
finding the article is partly about — the draft's thesis is that clusters are
cheap but unavailable, and the transport point it most wants is itself an
instance of that.
